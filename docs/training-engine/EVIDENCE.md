# Evidence and policy register

Reviewed 2026-09-05. This is a focused engineering literature review, not a preregistered systematic review. Primary journal abstracts and accessible full texts were checked. Some publisher full texts were unavailable; no hidden tables or precise dose-response coefficients are claimed. ACSM 2026 searched through October 2024; Pelland's later synthesis is considered separately. Healthy adults are the supported population, with many underlying trials disproportionately young and male. Confidence here describes evidence interpretation, not a calibrated probability of individual response.

| Variable | Starting policy / range | Goals | Evidence / population | Confidence and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | Beginner starts below trained target; hypertrophy generally around 10 effective sets for major groups, configurable ranges | All | [ACSM 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/), overview of 137 reviews, healthy adults | Strong direction; exact per-muscle numbers and novice ramp are engineering policy |
| Indirect work | Direct 1, meaningful indirect 0.5; incidental 0 | All | [Pelland et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), 67 studies, 2,058 participants, mostly young men | Fractional model fit strongest; 0.5 is an accounting approximation, not each exercise's measured stimulus |
| Diminishing returns | Reward approaches target then penalizes excess | All | Pelland | Supported direction; objective curve is heuristic, not a fitted biological model |
| Frequency | Distribute volume across available slots; favor repeated specific practice for strength | All | ACSM; Pelland | Frequency benefits depend on goal and volume; no unconditional twice-weekly requirement for every small muscle |
| Effort | Usually 2 RIR; novice/general work 3; no mandatory failure | All | [Robinson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2), exploratory meta-regressions | Hypertrophy associated with closer failure; exact optimal RIR uncertain; estimates in original studies were inferred |
| Load / reps | Heavy compound strength ranges, moderate compound hypertrophy and higher accessory ranges | Strength/hypertrophy | [Load meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35015560/), ACSM | Heavy loads favor maximal strength; hypertrophy can occur over broad loads. Exact rep bins are practical policy |
| Rest | Compound 120–180s; accessory 60–90s; never compressed to satisfy time cap | All | [Singer 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11349676/) | Small benefit possible beyond short rests, heterogeneous evidence; exact seconds are policy |
| Exercise order | Explicit strength lift/priority first, mechanics as tie-break | All | [Nunes 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/) | Strength benefits for exercises earlier; no convincing hypertrophy order effect |
| Equipment | Treat compatible modalities as useful; preserve test specificity | All | [Machines/free weights review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34609100/) | No universal muscle-building superiority of free weights |
| ROM | Comfortable full ROM default; no automatic specialized partials | All | [ROM review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34170576/), [2025 trained-subject trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959841/) | Lengthened partial evidence developing; exercise-level ROM metadata unavailable |
| Variation | Stable repeated movements; substitutions for constraints/coverage | All | [Kassiano 2022 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/) | Systematic variation may help; random novelty not warranted |
| Periodization | Stable multiweek template, observed-performance adaptation | All | [Moesgaard 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/), ACSM | Possible strength benefit, especially trained; hypertrophy superiority not established; competition peaking deferred |
| Autoregulation | Conservative observed RIR and repeated performance feedback | All | [Autoregulation review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35038063/) | Methods differ; double-progression and coarse-load-grid thresholds are versioned product policy |
| Deload | No calendar trigger; optional reduced volume after repeated decline + low readiness | All | [Delphi consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37730925/), [deload RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/) | Consensus and limited trials, not universal trigger validation |
| Warmup | Brief general allowance plus compound rehearsal/ramp sets | All | [2025 warmup review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39864808/) | No single optimal protocol; timings and percentages are conservative heuristics |
| Age | Adult eligibility; no age multiplier | All | ACSM healthy adulthood overview | Age alone does not justify a fixed reduction coefficient |
| Detraining | Stale history resets load calibration, gradual return volume | All | [Halonen 2024 trial](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/10024/161066/1/Scandinavian_Med_Sci_Sports_-_2024_-_Halonen_-_Does_Taking_a_Break_Matter_Adaptations_in_Muscle_Strength_and_Size_Between.pdf) | Loss/regain varies; no percentage loss per day inferred |
| e1RM | Epley/Brzycki only low-rep external-load sets; report confidence | Strength analytics | [Validation study](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/TREINO%20DE%20FORC%CC%A7A/Avaliac%CC%A7a%CC%83o/Validation%20of%20Submaximal%20Prediction%20Equations%20for%20the%201RM%20Bench%20Press%20Test.pdf) | Exercise/population specificity limits generalization; estimates never demographic predictions |
| Endurance | Higher rep targets, modest rests and load calibration | Muscular endurance | ACSM overview | Outcome supported; exact optimum less established than strength/hypertrophy |
| Power | Unsupported capability in V1 | Power | ACSM supports fast concentric/moderate loads | Dataset lacks velocity intent, coaching prerequisites and validated power suitability; do not fake support |
| Fat loss | General resistance policy; no energy deficit advice | Fat-loss support | Scope/product policy | Does not prescribe nutrition or predict fat loss |
| Fatigue/recovery | Decayed recent sets with 48h half-life, relative scheduling penalty only | All | Engineering heuristic | No validated universal equation for percentage muscle recovery; no medical interpretation |
| Adherence | Use actual completion to lower ambitious targets; never invent completed work | All | Engineering/product policy informed by feasibility | Missing logs do not establish missed training unless coverage is explicitly complete |

All exact optimizer weights, timing assumptions, volume ranges, upper guards and progression thresholds are versioned in `src/training/policy.ts`. Changing them requires a new policy version and regression tests. They are not scientific constants. Height and body mass are validated optional context and do not determine training loads or volume. No sex/BMI coefficients exist.
